# User jobs to be done: what people actually ask a Mac system monitor (research notes)

> Coreography research notes. Sources accessed 2026-10-17.

Scope: mining Apple Support Communities, MacRumors forums, Apple Stack Exchange, Hacker News, TidBITS Talk, Mac-Forums, GitHub issues, and articles (9to5Mac, Macworld, Eclectic Light, AppleInsider, MacRumors, iMore-adjacent blogs) from 2023-2026, with older threads included where the same question persists to this day. Reddit itself blocks bulk access; Reddit evidence is cited via threads linked from fetched articles or via search-indexed thread content. Sections are ordered by the volume and recency of thread evidence found in this survey: a count of what was found, not a measured incidence ranking. Where a claim could not be confirmed it is marked (unverified).

## Summary

Forum figures below are as-reported anecdotes, not measurements of
incidence or independently established root causes. Blocked and
single-source checks are recorded in the verification log.

- The dominant job is diagnostic, not observational: people open Activity Monitor with a question ("why is my Mac slow / hot / draining / full?") or a name ("what is `mediaanalysisd` and is it safe?"). They want a culprit and a verdict, not a graph.
- A small cast of Apple daemons answers most threads: `kernel_task` (usually thermal management, though not every burst; see #2), `mediaanalysisd`/`photoanalysisd`/`photolibraryd` (photo and Apple Intelligence analysis), `mds_stores`/`corespotlightd`/`mdworker` (Spotlight), `WindowServer` (compositing), `cloudd`/`bird`/`nsurlsessiond` (iCloud), `backupd` (Time Machine), `powerd`/`dasd` (power/scheduling), plus browser helpers (`Google Chrome Helper`, `Safari Web Content`) and VMs (`com.apple.Virtualization.VirtualMachine`, `qemu`, `com.docker.hyperkit`).
- The same questions recur every release and get amplified by each new macOS bug: Sequoia's runaway Spotlight writes, Tahoe's System Data growth and Electron/`_cornerMask` WindowServer leak, macOS 26.5's `mediaanalysisd` beachball wave (9to5Mac reports in June and July 2026).
- Activity Monitor's recurring failures, stated by users: no temperatures, fans, watts or throttle state (so `kernel_task` stays a mystery); no history (cannot answer "what happened overnight" or "what used my battery"); helper processes unattributed to their app (`nsurlsessiond`, `Safari Web Content`, Chrome's helper swarm); cumulative network counters with no live per-app rate or per-day totals; a hidden "Preventing Sleep" column that shows only the current instant; and no explanation of what any process is, whether it is normal, or whether it is safe to quit.
- "System Data" is a perennial unanswerable category: Storage settings gives one opaque bar, and users must reach for DaisyDisk/`du` to find the real cause (runaway Spotlight index, `bird` caches, snapshots, a 34 GB `powerlog` database, VM images).
- Memory questions are chronic and widely misunderstood: users read "Memory Used" instead of Memory Pressure, panic over any nonzero swap, and hit the "out of application memory" dialog (leaky apps ballooning virtual memory page tables, or a nearly-full disk) then ask whether they should have bought more RAM.
- Battery questions split in two: overnight drain (DarkWake bursts, `tcpkeepalive`, push traffic, Bluetooth wakes, attached peripherals, apps holding assertions) and "which app is eating power right now" (Energy Impact, 12 hr Power). Neither is well answered today.
- Post-2024 additions to the usual cast: Apple Intelligence analysis daemons (`mediaanalysisd`, `knowledgeconstructiond`/`spotlightknowledged`) and Electron apps taxing `WindowServer` on Tahoe. The list of daemons users must google keeps growing; a bundled plain-English process directory would retire a whole genre of threads.

## The questions, ranked by evidence

### 1. "Why is my Mac slow / beachballing / freezing?"

The umbrella question and the single most common reason anyone opens Activity Monitor. It splits into: sudden freezes and beach balls; everything-feels-sluggish; and the very common "slow right after a macOS update" variant, which is usually reindexing and resync but is sometimes a real bug (macOS 26's autofill-heuristic leak and the Electron `_cornerMask`/`WindowServer` GPU leak documented in 2026 troubleshooting guides).

- Example threads: [9to5Mac: beach balls and unresponsive trackpad on macOS 26.5](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/20/mac-beach-balls-and-unresponsive-trackpad-this-fix-seems-to-work/) (Jul 2026); [9to5Mac: AI photo scanning may be the reason](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/03/mac-beachballs-or-lagging-performance-ai-photo-scanning-may-be-the-reason/) (Jun 2026); [HomeTechOps runbook: slow after Tahoe/27 update](https://hometechops.com/mac/mac-slow-after-macos-tahoe-update) (2026); [Apple Discussions: slow after Tahoe upgrade](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256243424) (Feb 2026); [MacRumors Forums: Beach Balling! Why?](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/beach-balling-why.2348904/); [TidBITS Talk: That Spinning Beachball problem](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/that-spinning-beachball-problem/5089); [Apple Discussions: Slow iMac / unknown processes](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/252106488).
- Typical culprits named: `mediaanalysisd`, `corespotlightd`, `kernel_task`, `mds_stores`/`mdworker` (post-update reindex), `WindowServer`, browser tabs/helpers, Docker/VMs, a nearly-full disk, failing external drives, third-party "cleaner" and antivirus agents, and leaky apps driving "out of memory" dialogs.
- What answers it: per-process CPU over time (`proc_pidinfo`/`proc_pid_rusage`), memory pressure and swap (`host_statistics64`, `kern.memorystatus_vm_pressure_level`), disk throughput (`IOBlockStorageDriver`), and crucially a history that reaches back to when the freeze happened. Data sources: notes 09/10.
- Activity Monitor fails: no history, so a transient 30-second freeze is gone before you can look at it; no correlation of CPU, memory pressure and disk on one timeline; no "what changed in the last hour"; no plain-language verdict ("this is post-update indexing, it will finish").

### 2. "What is kernel_task and why is it eating 500-1000% CPU?"

One of the oldest and most-asked process questions; Apple maintains a dedicated support page for it. Apple's documented answer (thermal management: the kernel makes the CPU less available when something is too hot) explains most cases but not all: high `kernel_task` also appears with non-thermal triggers such as driver or I/O loops, external monitors and SMB copy loops, so "kernel_task is busy" should read "probably throttling, check thermal state" rather than "throttling". Either way the cause is invisible inside Activity Monitor, which only shows the process at the top of the CPU list while the Mac crawls.

- Example threads: [Apple Support: If kernel_task is using a large percentage of your Mac CPU](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102172) (updated Mar 2025); [Apple SE: How to find cause of high kernel_task CPU usage](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/363337/how-to-find-cause-of-high-kernel-task-cpu-usage) (the famous left-port-charging sensor finding); [MacRumors: Runaway Kernel Task on M1 MacBook Pro](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/runaway-kernel-task-on-m1-macbook-pro.2288846/) (2021, 1500-5000%); [Apple Discussions: kernel_task uses all cores with external monitors](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/252806193); [MacRumors: kernel_task stays maxed after demanding apps](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/kernel_task-stays-to-the-maximum-every-time-i-do-anything-demanding-even-after-closing-the-app.2294440/); [HN discussion of the mechanism](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47552414) (2026); [iFixit: kernel_task 500% after battery replacement](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/700197/kernel_task+using+500%25+of+CPU.+how+to+deactivate+thermal+throttling).
- Typical culprits named: heat from charging (especially left-side USB-C ports plus peripherals on Intel MacBooks), external monitors, hot chassis/ambient, dust/failing fans and sensors, demanding apps, and on Apple Silicon less often (SMB-share copy loops were one reported trigger).
- What answers it: temperatures (SMC keys), fan RPM (SMC), thermal pressure state (`ProcessInfo.thermalState`), CPU speed limit/throttle state (`pmset -g therm`, CLPC data), plus which process was actually burning CPU just before the throttle. Data sources: notes 09.
- Activity Monitor fails: no temperature, fan, watts or "currently throttling" indicator anywhere; `kernel_task` looks like a runaway process rather than a symptom. Users routinely try to force-quit it and ask how to disable thermal protection entirely.

### 3. "Why is my Mac hot / why are the fans so loud?"

Closely tied to #2 but asked separately: fans at full speed with modest CPU, a hot chassis, or the first-ever audible fan on an Apple Silicon MacBook. A sub-question that recurs constantly: "how do I check my CPU temperature", which has no built-in answer.

- Example threads: [Apple Discussions: overheating after Sequoia update](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255766180) (2024-2025); [Apple Discussions: loud fan with low CPU](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255288782); [Macworld: how to check your Mac's temperature](https://www.macworld.com/article/351897/how-to-check-mac-temperature-cool.html); [Apple SE: how to check die temperatures on Apple Silicon](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/476056/how-to-check-die-temperatures-on-an-apple-silicon-mac); [LidRun: reading real CPU temperature](https://lidrun.com/blog/read-mac-cpu-temperature-intel-apple-silicon) (Jun 2026, documents that macOS ships no temperature display at all); [DisplayLink forum: DisplayLinkUserAgent CPU spike on external display](https://www.displaylink.org/forum/showthread.php?t=68210).
- Typical culprits named: post-update background work (indexing, Photos analysis, Time Machine catch-up), a single web tab or incompatible app (the Sequoia thread's Canon camera app), `kernel_task` masking the real hog, DisplayLink drivers, Chrome/Electron apps, ambient heat and blocked vents.
- What answers it: SMC temperature and fan keys, IOReport power (watts per cluster/GPU), `ProcessInfo.thermalState`, per-process energy (`proc_pid_rusage`). Note the fragility: sensor keys change per chip (Tp/Te/Tf/Tg prefixes moved across M1-M5; gated cores report garbage ~2°C values) per the LidRun article and Stats/iStat maintenance notes in notes 02/09.
- Activity Monitor fails: zero thermal data; Energy Impact is Apple's suggested proxy but does not price E vs P core work correctly on Apple Silicon (notes 01). Users must install a third-party tool just to see a temperature.

### 4. "What are mediaanalysisd / photoanalysisd / photolibraryd doing, and can I stop them?"

A huge, recurring wave: Photos and Apple Intelligence media analysis hammering CPU for days, often overnight or during sleep, and eating battery. Users are suspicious by name ("analysis"), want to know if it is spying on them, how long it will run, and how to kill it; the standard correct answer is "let it finish on AC power, it will stop".

- Example threads: [Apple SE: Why is mediaanalysisd taking a lot of CPU](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/477679/why-is-the-mediaanalysisd-taking-a-lot-of-cpu-on-macos) (M4; notes mds_stores also writing hundreds of GB, resolved in 15.3); [MacRumors: mediaanalysisd, photoanalysisd and photolibraryd](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/mediaanalysisd-photoanalysisd-and-photolibraryd.2445597/) (2024-2025, incl. the `ResourceIntensive` launchd flag discussion and launchctl disable instructions); [AppleInsider: how to stop mediaanalysisd hogging CPU](https://appleinsider.com/inside/macos-ventura/tips/how-to-stop-mediaanalysisd-from-hogging-your-cpu-in-macos) (Feb 2023); [Apple Discussions: mediaanalysisd high CPU during sleep](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256129987) (Sep 2025); [Apple Discussions: mediaanalysisd in Catalina](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/250866713); [9to5Mac: AI photo scanning may be the reason](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/03/mac-beachballs-or-lagging-performance-ai-photo-scanning-may-be-the-reason/) (Jun 2026); [macsecurity.net: photoanalysisd profile](https://macsecurity.net/view/661-photoanalysisd-high-cpu-process-macos) (Nov 2025); [Apple Discussions: list of mystery processes incl. mediaanalysisd at 83%](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254328150).
- Typical culprits named: the daemons themselves (no external culprit; triggers are large or new Photos libraries, OS updates, iCloud Photos sync, stuck/corrupt items looping, and apparently Apple Intelligence-driven reanalysis waves in 2025-2026).
- What answers it: which daemon and how much CPU over time (`proc_pid_rusage`), that it runs at background/maintenance QoS and is observed mostly confined to E cores (the same pattern Eclectic Light documented for `backupd`; QoS biases scheduling toward E cores but does not guarantee placement), plus progress scope (Photos' People scan counter; unified log / `dasd` task records).
- Activity Monitor fails: no progress or ETA, no "this is normal and bounded" signal, no way to see it only runs when idle/on AC, and CPU Time accumulation during sleep is invisible (users catch it only via iStat or a lucky glimpse). Force-quit just respawns it.

### 5. "Why is Spotlight burning CPU/disk (mds_stores / corespotlightd / mdworker)?"

Perennial, and sharply worse recently: post-update reindexing is the standard benign cause, but 2024-2026 produced genuine runaway bugs (Sequoia dev beta writing terabytes, Tahoe System Data growth) plus chronic stuck-index cases (corespotlightd at 100-600% for weeks).

- Example threads: [Apple SE: mds and mds_stores constantly consuming CPU](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/144474/mds-and-mds-stores-constantly-consuming-cpu) (incl. the `fs_usage` trick to see what is being indexed); [Apple Developer Forums: corespotlightd constant 100% CPU on Monterey](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/716484) (user let it run two weeks, never finished); [macReports: corespotlightd high CPU on Sequoia](https://macreports.com/corespotlightd-causing-high-cpu-usage-on-macos-sequoia-how-to-fix-it/) (May 2025); [MacRumors: severe Spotlight reindexing problems, root cause](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/severe-spotlight-reindexing-problems-root-cause.2480836/) (deep instrumentation: 13.7M pending FSEvents, QoS 9 confining it to E cores, ~90-minute heartbeat gaps making progress invisible); [Apple Discussions: mds_stores at 99.5%](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/251490995) and [mds_stores at 600%](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253598560); [mjtsai: Spotlight Indexing Running Wild](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/07/21/spotlight-indexing-running-wild/) (Jul 2025, links the [original Reddit thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/MacOSBeta/comments/1dfo2sl/ridiculously_high_disk_write_rate_from_unknown/)); [notebookcheck: Sequoia Spotlight SSD woes](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Spotlight-bug-reportedly-responsible-for-macOS-Sequoia-SSD-woes.852109.0.html).
- Typical culprits named: `mds_stores`, `corespotlightd`, `mdworker_shared`, `fileproviderd` racing it, stuck Pages/iCloud Drive documents, indexing Time Machine volumes and external drives, Xcode-derived folders.
- What answers it: which files it is chewing (`fs_usage -w -f filesys mds`), index size on disk (`.Spotlight-V100`), FSEvents backlog, and md* process presence plus CPU burst as the practical "indexing now" signal. Note `mdutil -s` reports only whether indexing is *enabled* per volume ("Indexing enabled."), not whether it is actively running or how far along it is; there is no documented progress API (the Spotlight "Indexing…" banner covers only some active indexing). Data sources: FSEvents, filesystem stats, unified log; notes 10.
- Activity Monitor fails: shows the CPU but not the subject (which files/directories trigger the loop), no indexing progress or "working vs stuck" distinction, no view of the index's disk footprint, and nothing linking the disk-write rate to Spotlight at all.

### 6. "Why is WindowServer using so much CPU and memory?"

Classic compositor complaint, supercharged by external displays: CPU spikes when moving the mouse, memory climbing to 3-75 GB over days, forced reboots. Tahoe added a new variant: Electron apps' `_cornerMask` GPU leak and Liquid Glass compositing load.

- Example threads: [Apple SE: WindowServer high CPU using external monitor](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/407177/windowserver-high-cpu-using-external-monitor-big-sur) (gaming mouse, Night Shift and unaccelerated display anecdotes); [MacRumors: WindowServer 3GB with dual 4K + Sidecar](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/windowserver-using-3gb-when-connected-to-dual-4k-displays-and-sidecar.2353772/); [Apple Discussions: WindowServer memory leak to 75 GB](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255632112) (root-caused to a 165 Hz display mode); [MacRumors: WindowServer memory leak on Ventura](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/windowserver-memory-leak.2381649/) (33 GB in 8 h); [macsecurity.net: WindowServer high CPU explainer](https://macsecurity.net/view/393-windowserver-mac); [HomeTechOps Tahoe guide](https://hometechops.com/mac/mac-slow-after-macos-tahoe-update) (Electron/WindowServer GPU leak, Liquid Glass load).
- Typical culprits named: external monitors (especially 4K+, high-refresh, scaled resolutions, DisplayLink docks), transparency effects, many Spaces and windows, cluttered desktops, Night Shift transitions, high-polling-rate gaming mice, Electron apps on Tahoe.
- What answers it: WindowServer CPU/RSS over time (root-owned, so root or Endpoint Security for full detail; notes 10), display configuration (IOKit/CGDisplay), per-process GPU time (AGX registry), and history to catch the growth curve.
- Activity Monitor fails: WindowServer memory is split into opaque "Memory" vs "Real Memory" columns that confused the 75 GB reporter; no display-attach correlation; no per-display or per-app window cost attribution; nothing tells you "plugging in that 165 Hz panel is the cause".

### 7. "Memory pressure is yellow/red, swap is used, 'out of application memory', do I need more RAM?"

The chronic confusion package: users misread "Memory Used" as a problem, panic at any swap, hit the system OOM dialog, and ask purchase-advice. Also the 8 GB vs 16 GB debate that ran through the entire M1 era and the SSD-swap scare.

- Example threads: [9to5Mac: 'Your system has run out of application memory'](https://9to5mac.com/2021/11/01/your-system-has-run-out-of-application-memory/) (Nov 2021, Mail/Final Cut leaking past 100 GB); [Apple Discussions: OOM dialog with 16 GB free-looking RAM](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253310137) (explains page-table exhaustion vs low disk); [Apple Discussions: OOM on Tahoe 26.3.1](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256262139) (Mar 2026); [Apple Discussions: M2 Air OOM with only Finder running](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255397869); [SuperUser: do I need more RAM](https://superuser.com/questions/1682576/do-i-need-more-ram) (swap-used confusion); [Apple Discussions: if memory pressure is low do I need more RAM](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/6418565); [MPU Talk: do I need more RAM](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/do-i-need-more-ram/9458); [Apple guide: check if your Mac needs more RAM](https://support.apple.com/guide/activity-monitor/check-if-your-mac-needs-more-ram-actmntr34865/mac) (Apple's own answer is "watch Memory Pressure", seen in the fetched guide TOC).
- Typical culprits named: browser tabs (each a process), Electron apps, Mail/Final Cut leaks, WindowServer itself (see #6), Docker/VMs, and "nothing running" cases that turn out to be leaks or low free disk blocking swapfiles.
- What answers it: memory pressure and composition (`host_statistics64`: free/wired/compressed/cached, `kern.memorystatus_vm_pressure_level`), swap used and swapins/swapouts over time (`vm_stat`, 16 KB pages on Apple Silicon per Eclectic Light), per-process memory (`proc_pid_rusage`), and free disk for swapfile growth.
- Activity Monitor fails: Memory Pressure is a good single idea but a tiny unexplained graph; swap shows only the current instant, not history (you cannot see that 20 GB was swapped during the Time Machine run); "Memory Used" vs cached invites misreading; nothing maps the OOM dialog to a culprit at all: page-table/address-space exhaustion is one reported mechanism (per the linked Apple Discussions thread), and a large virtual-size figure alone does not prove it; sparse reservations inflate virtual size harmlessly, so the signal is resident+compressed+swap growth.

### 8. "What drained my battery overnight, and what is stopping my Mac sleeping?"

Two joined questions: waking to a dead or diminished MacBook, and the Mac that will not stay asleep. Root causes are power assertions, DarkWake activity (push, iCloud sync, Bluetooth, network) and attached peripherals.

- Example threads: [MacRumors: battery draining 30% during sleep overnight](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/battery-draining-30-during-sleep-overnight.2447375/) (Jan 2025, `pmset` output included); [MacRumors: M1 MacBook battery drain in sleep](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/m1-macbook-batterydrain-in-sleep.2346759/) (Intel slept a month, M1 dies in days; hibernatemode discussion); [MacRumors article: macOS 12.2 Bluetooth battery drain in sleep](https://www.macrumors.com/2022/01/30/macos-12-2-bluetooth-battery-drain/) (100% to 0% overnight via DarkWake); [Apple SE: what does my Mac do while sleeping all night](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/423593/what-does-my-mac-do-while-sleeping-all-night-long) (`pmset -g log` DarkWake analysis); [Apple SE: MacBook Air battery drain while sleeping](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/470712/macbook-air-battery-drain-while-sleeping) (EC.ARPT push bursts on port 5223); [Apple SE: find what program is preventing sleep](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/364622/how-can-i-find-what-program-is-preventing-my-mac-from-going-to-sleep) (Preventing Sleep column + `pmset -g assertions`); [CodeJam: macOS won't sleep from the Apple menu](https://www.codejam.info/2023/04/macos-wont-sleep.html) (assertions log detective story); [Apple Discussions: Sequoia 15.5 drains battery fast](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256067760) (ChatGPT app culprit); [Apple Discussions: M1 Air drain tests per peripheral](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/252191369).
- Typical culprits named: `tcpkeepalive`/Power Nap and push traffic, Bluetooth accessories, external monitors and USB-C dongles, browsers and Electron apps (Chrome, ChatGPT), widgets, `cloudphotosd` darkwake sync, mediaanalysisd running during sleep, `sharingd`/`coreaudiod` assertions, `powerd`, `WindowServer` UserIsActive.
- What answers it: sleep/power assertions per process (`IOPMCopyAssertionsByProcess`, the source behind `pmset -g assertions`), wake/DarkWake history (`pmset -g log`, unified log power domain), per-process energy (`proc_pid_rusage`, and the system's own powerlog database; see notes 09/10).
- Activity Monitor fails: a "Preventing Sleep" column exists but is off by default, current-instant only, and gives no reason string (pmset shows the named assertion); Energy tab shows 12 hr app power but not *who woke the machine* or the overnight timeline; no assertion history at all.

### 9. "What is 'System Data' and why is it eating my disk?"

The most opaque storage question on the platform: a grey bar of tens to hundreds of GB that Apple defines as "files that don't fit other categories" and explicitly says you cannot manage. 2025-2026 made it worse (Tahoe-era Spotlight loops filling drives invisible to DaisyDisk).

- Example threads: [MacRumors: 'System Data' consumes 142 GB, unable to figure out why](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/system-data-consumes-142-gb-of-space-unable-to-figure-out-why.2456056/) (Apr 2025, dropped to 51 GB after a point update); [Apple Discussions: System Data uses 218 GB](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255084458) (incl. Apple's "cannot manage" definition); [Apple Discussions: 169 GB System Data](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255787059) (Spotlight suspect); [Apple Discussions: System Data growing unchecked](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253863859) (OP-reported figure in the hundreds of GB; root-caused in the thread to `bird` cache files under `~/Library/Caches/CloudKit/com.apple.bird`); [mjtsai: Tahoe Free Space Problems](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/08/19/tahoe-free-space-problems/) (multiple reports: 300-384 GB, mds_stores leak, disk "full" with invisible usage); [Eclectic Light: Storage settings explainer](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/12/20/explainer-storage-settings/) (Dec 2025: "System Data is the one category that desperately needs further information, but doesn't have a button"); [dev.to: a 34 GB powerlog file filled my disk on macOS 27 beta](https://dev.to/azaek/how-a-34-gb-log-filled-my-macs-disk-and-how-i-found-it-1d4j) (Oct 2026: `dasd` re-running App Store tasks at ~700 rows/s, `appstoreagent` at 148% CPU); [greggant: reclaiming System Data tutorial](https://blog.greggant.com/posts/2022/04/10/reclaiming-space-from-system-data-in-macos.html); [jonathansblog: 180 GB freed by rebuilding the Spotlight index](https://jonathansblog.co.uk/freeing-up-180gb-on-macos-by-rebuilding-the-spotlight-index) (Aug 2026, adapting an r/MacOS writeup).
- Typical culprits named: runaway `.Spotlight-V100`/`CoreSpotlight` indexes, `bird`/CloudKit caches, APFS local snapshots, `~/Library/Caches` and orphaned app data, VM/swapfiles, Docker images and simulators, iOS backups, logs (`powerlog`, diagnostics), and APFS accounting bugs where "used" space shows in no folder at all.
- What answers it: per-volume used/free with snapshots and purgeable space (APFS/`diskutil`), folder-size scan (the DaisyDisk/du method), Spotlight index size, deleted-but-open files (`lsof +L1`). Data sources: notes 10/11.
- Activity Monitor fails: the Disk tab shows I/O rates and bytes, not capacity attribution. Storage settings shows the category but not its composition, which is the actual question. Users end up in Terminal or paying for DaisyDisk to answer it.

### 10. "What is writing hundreds of GB to my SSD, and is swap killing it?"

Born in the March 2021 M1 swap scare and kept alive by real bugs: people watch Activity Monitor's "Data Written" climb by tens of GB while idle and ask whether the SSD is being eaten, by what, and whether their 8 GB Mac is swap-murdering itself.

- Example threads: [MacRumors: tweaks/fixes list for high SSD writes on M1](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/a-list-of-tweaks-fixes-to-try-if-youre-experiencing-high-ssd-writes-on-m1.2293378/) (reduced 350-500 GB/day to 25-50 GB; Safari caching and tab discarding named top culprit); [MacRumors: what causes excessive writes to SSD](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/what-causes-excessive-writes-to-ssd-on-macos.2413969/) (Dec 2023, 44 GB in two days of browsing); [Apple Discussions: Sequoia bug wearing out SSDs](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255870963) (1.5 TB written in days, culprit `mds_stores`); [Eclectic Light: tracking swap space and SSD wear](https://eclecticlight.co/2022/12/02/tracking-swap-space-is-it-wearing-out-your-ssd/) (vm_stat swapins/outs method); [HN: kernel_task disk writes crazy high with Rosetta](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26246137) (hundreds of GB/hour); [Apple SE mediaanalysisd thread](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/477679/why-is-the-mediaanalysisd-taking-a-lot-of-cpu-on-macos) (mds_stores writing "hundreds of gigabytes" in 8 h); [Apple Discussions: swap concern on M2 Air](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255604027).
- Typical culprits named: swap/VM (`backupd` alone ballooning swap to 12-20 GB during backups per [this Apple SE thread](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/350111/why-does-backupd-cause-excessive-tens-of-gb-swap-memory-usage-when-doing-a-tim)), Safari's website cache, `mds_stores`/Spotlight runaway, `kernel_task` (Rosetta-era writes), browser caches, backup agents, Photos analysis.
- What answers it: per-process bytes read/written (`proc_pid_rusage`), system write rate (`IOBlockStorageDriver`), swap totals (`vm_stat`), SMART wear indicators via NVMe SMART (limited on Apple SSDs per the DriveDx discussion in Eclectic Light). Data sources: notes 10.
- Activity Monitor fails: per-process Disk columns exist but silently leave gaps (the reference screenshot in notes 00 shows an empty per-process list while the system reports 83 MB/s), no cumulative-per-day attribution, no breakdown of swap vs file I/O writes, and no SMART/wear context to answer the real fear.

### 11. "Which app or process is using my network / uploading right now?"

The metered-connection and "why is my upload light blinking" question. The honest answers live in `nettop` and Little Snitch; Activity Monitor's Network tab shows totals but no live per-app rate, and attributes traffic to helpers (`nsurlsessiond`) rather than the app you recognize.

- Example threads: [Apple SE: how can I see what bandwidth each app or process is using](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/16690/how-can-i-see-what-bandwidth-each-app-or-process-is-using) (the canonical `nettop -P -d` answer, still the top hit); [Apple Discussions: cloudd & nsurlsessiond consuming all bandwidth](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/6985232) (gigabytes per hour clogging the LAN; iCloud Photos pause fixed it); [MacRumors: nsurlsessiond downloading nonstop](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/nsurlsessiond-downloading-nonstop-in-the-background.2449157/) (Feb 2025, 35-200 kB/s mystery traffic, `tasks.sqlite` forensics); [Apple Discussions: cloudd 100-200% CPU](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/252510404) (shared iCloud folder); [Apple Discussions: icloud services destroying cpu](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/8327918) (`bird`+`cloudd` >90%); [Bytetally: which app is uploading data](https://bytetally.app/en/which-app-is-uploading-data-mac/) (Jul 2026; documents exactly where the built-in tools fall short); [OS X Daily: nettop](https://osxdaily.com/2013/06/07/watch-network-traffic-mac-os-x-nettop/); [bandwidther](https://github.com/simonw/bandwidther) (a menu-bar app built specifically because this question lacks a GUI answer).
- Typical culprits named: `nsurlsessiond`, `cloudd`, `bird` (iCloud sync/upload), `appstoreagent`/`softwareupdated` (updates), Photos upload, Dropbox/Drive/OneDrive helpers, backup services, browser tabs, `mDNSResponder`.
- What answers it: per-process live network rate and totals (private NetworkStatistics framework as `nettop` uses; notes 10), plus per-connection endpoints (`lsof -i` style data) to identify *what* it is sending to.
- Activity Monitor fails: Sent/Rcvd Bytes are cumulative since process start (no per-day answer), no live per-process rate column, helper-attribution hides the real app, VPN/proxy makes it worse (everything attributed to the VPN process), zero history and no remote-endpoint view.

### 12. "What is this process, is it safe, can I quit it?"

The identification job: an unfamiliar name at the top of the list (`rpcsvchost`, `coreauthd`, `InterCheck`, `sharingd`, `accountsd`, `distnoted`, a vendor-named helper), the fear it is malware, and "what happens if I force-quit it". How-To Geek built an entire still-cited article series on this single question.

- Example threads: [How-To Geek: What is this process and why is it running (index of 23 process explainers)](https://www.howtogeek.com/312671/what-is-this-process-and-why-is-it-running-on-my-mac/); [Apple SE: InterCheck at 96% CPU](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/44740/what-is-the-intercheck-process-that-occasionally-uses-96-cpu-on-my-mac) (Sophos AV scanner); [Apple Discussions: mystery "-I" process](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/250013382) (turned out to be Malwarebytes RTProtectionDaemon); [Apple Discussions: sharingd](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/252018060); [Apple Discussions: list of never-seen processes](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254328150); [MacRumors guide: accountsd high CPU](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/accountsd/) (400%+ wave on Catalina); [macsecurity.net WindowServer page](https://macsecurity.net/view/393-windowserver-mac) (exists precisely because users google "is WindowServer a virus"); [AppAddict: ProcessSpy after a year](https://appaddict.app/post/processspy-after-a-year) (Jun 2026: Activity Monitor "is remarkably bad at answering the one question you actually have: what is this thing and why is it doing that"; click-to-identify, signature/entitlement inspector, $34.99).
- Typical culprits named: Apple daemons mistaken for malware, antivirus/security agents themselves (Sophos InterCheck, Malwarebytes, ESET/BitDefender conflicts), browser updaters (Google Keystone), vendor helpers (Wacom, Zoom, Muse Hub), and actual adware borrowing legit-sounding names.
- What answers it: code signature/notarization and signing org, bundle id and path, parent process/launchd provenance, entitlements, install source (launchd plist, SMAppService registration). Data sources: `proc_pidpath`, SecCode/Notarization checks, ES; notes 10.
- Activity Monitor fails: shows name, PID, user and resource numbers only. No path, signature, parent chain, or "who installed this"; no explanation text; the Inspect panel requires power-user literacy. Force-quitting a system daemon just respawns it (or logs you out for WindowServer), which users learn the hard way.

### 13. "Which browser tab/helper is eating my CPU and RAM?"

Every browser multiplies into dozens of identically-named helpers (`Google Chrome Helper`, `Safari Web Content`, `Firefox Web Content`), and Activity Monitor cannot say which tab or extension any of them is. Users resort to killing PIDs one by one and watching what breaks.

- Example threads: [Stack Overflow: which tab is 'Google Chrome Helper' running on](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/35178987/which-tab-is-google-chrome-helper-running-on) (the canonical question; answer is Chrome's own Task Manager or binary-search killing); [Apple SE: why does Google Chrome Helper spike my CPU](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/138616/why-does-google-chrome-helper-spike-my-cpu) (fan-spinning tab/extension hunts); [Apple SE: Chrome Helper using too much memory](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/274647/google-chrome-helper-using-up-too-much-memory) ("a dozen instances"); [Apple SE: how to make Chrome Helper use less CPU](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/272000/how-to-make-google-chrome-helper-not-use-as-much-cpu); [Stack Overflow: get pid of Chrome tab](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/27679223/get-pid-of-chrome-tab-via-terminal).
- Typical culprits named: ad/script-heavy tabs (pinned web apps like Okta were named), extensions (Google Offline, Markdown Here, cryptominers found via Chrome Task Manager), `Safari Web Content` tab processes, `WallpaperVideoExtension`-style extras ([HN: Sonoma video wallpaper was the 3rd-highest CPU process](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38849894)).
- What answers it: the process tree rolled up per app (P20 pattern; iStat's Combine Processes does this) plus, for real tab-level answers, the browser's own task manager or per-process window-title/command-line inspection. Data sources: proc_pidinfo + process group/parent; notes 10.
- Activity Monitor fails: helpers are not folded into their app and carry no tab/title identity; the user cannot map "Google Chrome Helper (Renderer)" to a tab at all from macOS. A monitor can get halfway (per-app rollup, command line, GPU time) but true per-tab mapping needs browser cooperation; ProcessSpy and MacJet both advertise improved helper identification.

### 14. "What are these login items and background items, and can I disable them?"

Since Ventura split Login Items into "Open at Login" and "Allow in the Background" and started warning users about each addition, a steady question: what is `stklaunchagent.sh` / "Juuso Salonen" / Google Updater, why can't I remove the entry, and will disabling it break something. The Ventura "Background Items Added" notification bug (fixed in 13.3) burned the question into a whole user cohort.

- Example threads: [MacRumors article: Ventura 'Background Items Added' notification bug](https://www.macrumors.com/2023/02/02/macos-background-items-added-notification-bug/) (Feb 2023); [iDownloadBlog coverage incl. the 13.3 fix](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2023/02/14/mac-background-items-added-notification/); [MacRumors forum thread on the barrage](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/barrage-of-background-items-added-notifications.2377082/) (postgres example); [TidBITS Talk: Just upgraded to Ventura](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/just-upgraded-to-ventura/24906) (user's keep/disable reasoning: AutoUpdate yes, Creative Cloud no); [Apple Discussions: what is stklaunchagent.sh](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255282824) (Save to Kindle); [Mac-Forums: background items can't be removed](https://www.mac-forums.com/threads/background-items-cant-remove-from-list.375248/) (orphaned CleanMyMac/Luxoft entries); [Mac-Help: 'Juuso Salonen' popup](https://www.mac-help.com/threads/juuso-salonen-pop-up.229862/) (developer's real name with no app name attached).
- Typical culprits named: Google Updater/Keystone, Adobe CC helpers, Dropbox, Microsoft AutoUpdate, Malwarebytes, CCC helper, VirtualBox, Wireshark, XQuartz, postgres, Kindle and other vendor agents, plus ghost entries left after uninstall.
- What answers it: the launchd/SMAppService registration for each item, the owning app and signature, and the live resource cost of the running agent (the join: this background item belongs to that app and currently costs X CPU/memory). Data sources: launchd plists, SMAppService/BTM, Endpoint Security; notes 10/11.
- Activity Monitor fails: no link between a running helper and its login-item registration; System Settings shows the item but not what it does, who signed it, what it costs, or whether disabling breaks the app (CCC's helper is a named example where it does).

### 15. "How is my game/app performing (FPS, GPU load, thermals)?"

Smaller on Mac than Windows but persistent, and growing with Apple silicon gaming: "why is this game stuttering", "how do I see FPS", "is the GPU even being used". There is no consumer-visible FPS or per-app GPU frame display anywhere in macOS.

- Example threads: [iGeeksBlog: how to check FPS in games on Mac](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-check-fps-in-games-on-mac/) (four methods: Steam counter, in-game counters, `MTL_HUD_ENABLED` via launchctl, Quartz Debug FrameMeter); [TheDroidGuy: check FPS on Mac](https://thedroidguy.com/how-to-check-your-fps-in-games-on-mac-1262565); [Apple SE: interpreting spindump FPS report](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/430213/how-to-interpret-fps-report-in-a-spindump-output) (Activity Monitor's hidden frame-rate report in diagnostics); [Apple guide: view GPU activity](https://support.apple.com/guide/activity-monitor/view-gpu-activity-actm9329b315/mac) (exists only as a separate GPU History window; seen in fetched guide TOC).
- Typical culprits named: Rosetta-translated or unoptimized builds, thermal throttling, wrong resolution/scaling, windowed vs fullscreen, other processes competing, external-display refresh issues.
- What answers it: GPU utilization/memory (`IOAccelerator` stats) and per-process GPU time (AGX registry), thermal pressure, and FPS/frame times (on macOS 27, `metalperftrace listen --pid --json` streams live per-process frame statistics at 1 s intervals, verified against the macOS 27.0 man page and shipping third-party code in notes 05/09/14; before 27 the only route is Metal HUD opt-in).
- Activity Monitor fails: the GPU History window is a separate utility window with total utilization only; no per-process GPU column, no FPS for any app, nothing in-window. Apple's consumer Game Overlay (macOS 26) shows no performance data, which is exactly the gap (notes 05).

## Implications for Coreography

The ranking doubles as a product spec. Ordered by what the evidence says users need most:

1. **Answer the question, literally.** Most people open a monitor with one of fifteen questions. A "Why is my Mac slow / hot / draining / full?" surface that names the current culprit with a one-line verdict would be the app's identity. TMOG had the right framing; the content must go further.
2. **Ship a plain-English daemon dictionary.** Explain recurring processes through verified identity, a curated role description and observed activity. Avoid promising duration or quit safety from a name alone. This capability already exists: note 13 documents WhySlow, SystemWatch, TaskExplorer and MacStats. Activity Monitor lacks it; Coreography's opportunity is joining explanations to visual evidence and history, not claiming an exclusive dictionary feature.
3. **History is the feature that answers the most questions.** "What happened overnight", "what froze the Mac 30 seconds ago", "what was writing to disk while I was away" are all unanswerable anywhere on macOS today. Continuous local recording with scrub-back attribution is the single highest-leverage capability (P3/P6/P7).
4. **Put thermals next to the process list.** Temperature, fan RPM, watts and an explicit "throttling now" state turn the #2 and #3 questions into readings instead of guesses. On macOS 27 this needs the IOReport fallbacks from notes 09 plus explicit availability states.
5. **Sleep and battery deserve a real view.** Live per-process sleep assertions (with the named reason strings pmset shows), a wake/DarkWake timeline from the power log, and per-app energy over the day: this answers #8 end to end, and nothing else does.
6. **Per-app network must have a rate and a day-total.** Cumulative-since-launch counters are why `nsurlsessiond` threads never die. Live up/down rates per app, per-day totals, helper traffic folded into the owning app, and endpoint names on demand.
7. **Fold helpers into apps, then let users unfold.** `Safari Web Content`, `Google Chrome Helper`, `nsurlsessiond`, Electron helpers: the rolled-up app row is the answer users want ("Chrome is using 40%"), with children one disclosure away (P20). Tab-level identity should be offered where obtainable (window titles, command line) and honestly marked when not.
8. **Explain memory, don't just report it.** Memory Pressure with an inline definition, swap-over-time (not just now), and per-process resident/compressed/swap growth so the OOM dialog can be mapped to its likeliest culprit; virtual size alone is not proof, since sparse reservations inflate it harmlessly. "Do I need more RAM" is a verdict the app can compute: sustained red pressure + swap churn under the user's real workload.
9. **Open the black box of "System Data".** A storage card decomposing the category into snapshots, caches, Spotlight index, VM/swap, powerlog/logs and orphaned app data answers a question Storage settings structurally cannot. Observation only; deletion stays out of scope.
10. **Join background items to their cost.** A startup/login-items inventory linked to live CPU/memory and signing identity turns "can I disable this" into an informed decision instead of a coin flip (P23-adjacent).
11. **Say "normal, wait it out" when that is the answer.** A large fraction of correct forum responses are "let indexing/analysis finish on power". Where the system exposes real progress (Time Machine's `tmutil status` percent/ETA, Photos' analysis counters), show it; where only an enabled flag exists (`mdutil -s`), say "indexing enabled" rather than implying in-flight progress, and never promise a completion time a name alone can't support. A visible bounded job defuses the kill-the-daemon reflex.
12. **Expose QoS and throttle state per process.** `backupd` and `mds_stores` run at background QoS and are observed mostly confined to E cores (a scheduling bias, not a placement guarantee); showing that explains "why is this slow" better than a percentage does (P10 extends naturally to per-process QoS flags).
